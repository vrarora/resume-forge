# Interview Guide

The interview turns a person's memory into verified facts. Old resumes describe duties, while the interview finds the outcomes, defines every number and marks what's confidential.

## How to ask

- **Use rounds.** One round is at most 4 multiple-choice questions, or about 12 numbered fact questions grouped by role. Put the questions that block drafting first.
- **Use multiple choice (AskUserQuestion) for decisions**, such as market, target role, positioning, tone and outputs. Explain every option in its description, so the person never has to ask what an option means. Put the recommended option first and mark it "(Recommended)".
- **Use plain numbered questions for facts.** Short answers are fine. Say that answering in any order is fine.
- **Use what you already read.** Quote the case study or post you're asking about, for example "Your onboarding case study says activation rose to 40%. Over how many sign-ups?"
- **Advise when research answers the question.** If the person says "not sure, advise me", give a clear recommendation with the reason.
- **Accept "you decide."** Decide, then state the decision in one line so they can overrule it.

## facts.md format

Record every claim with its source. Every bullet in the resume must trace back to a line here.

```markdown
## Acme Corp · Senior Analyst · Mar 2023 – Present
- Cut month-end close from 9 days to 4 · source: interview round 2 · period: FY24 · team: led 3 analysts
- Won Analyst of the Year 2024 · source: LinkedIn award post
- CONFIDENTIAL: client names (use "a top-5 US bank")
- DISCREPANCY: resume says Mar 2023, LinkedIn says Apr 2023 → asked, answer: Apr 2023
```

## Round 0: discrepancies

Before round 1, list the conflicts you found between sources: dates, titles, locations and metrics. Fold them into round 2 or ask about them at the end of round 1.

## Round 1: direction (multiple choice)

1. **Market.** Local on-site, remote for global companies, or relocation. This changes currency, spelling, page size (A4 or Letter) and how much local jargon to explain.
2. **Target role and variant order.** Which role to optimise for first. Promise later variants instead of blending two roles into one weak resume.
3. **Breadth.** Stay in the current niche, broaden to the adjacent field, or stay fully open.
4. **Positioning and tone.** Which identity leads the headline, and whether the tone is builder or measured (see `writing.md`).

If the person asks for research before choosing, run it first and come back with a recommendation.

## Round 2: facts for each role (numbered)

For each recent role, ask:
- **What does each number measure?** Ask for the denominator, the period, what it includes, and whether it's ARR, contract value or pipeline.
- **What was the person's part?** Sole owner, lead, or contributor, plus team size.
- **Which projects happened under which title**, if they were promoted.
- **What's missing from the resume?** Products, launches and wins, each marked "led" or "contributed".
- **What's confidential?** Client names, internal product names, revenue figures.
- Every discrepancy from round 0.

## Round 3: gaps for the target role

- **Senior signals.** Mentoring, standards they set, cross-team adoption, disagreements they settled with evidence. Don't invent mentoring if they don't do it, because standard-setting counts too.
- **Awards.** The exact name, the period, and what each one was for.
- **Side projects** with users, revenue or press.
- **Tools** they could defend in an interview at the target company.

## Round for each variant

When you start a new variant, ask only what that role screens for and the current resume doesn't show. Founding roles screen for code stack, brand work and side projects. Management roles screen for team size, hiring and budget. Career changes screen for transferable outcomes and new credentials.

## Always ask once

- The contact details to show (phone format, which email), and whether to add a "remote / relocation" line. Default to leaving it off.
- Whether to add the graduation year.
- Whether the current employer might see the resume or LinkedIn, which affects how openly to signal a job search.

## Stop rule

Stop interviewing when both are true:
- Every bullet you plan to write traces back to `facts.md`.
- No open question would change what goes on the page.

Then say so in one line, with any judgment calls you made ("I'm leaving the 30% number in and the $4 ARPU out, because…"), and start drafting.
